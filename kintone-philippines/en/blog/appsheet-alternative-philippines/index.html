Google AppSheet alternative in the Philippines

By Tom Arai, Founder & CEO, Edamame Inc. · Updated 2026-09-20

Short answer: Teams usually look for an AppSheet alternative at a specific moment: the app outgrew its spreadsheet backend, approvals became multi-step, or the per-user model stopped fitting once people outside the company needed access. Kintone is built for that stage — a relational database, workflow and permissions in one platform, with a local Philippine partner for implementation and training. If you are still building simple internal apps on Google Sheets and already pay for Google Workspace, staying on AppSheet is the honest recommendation.

Why Philippine teams look for a Google AppSheet alternative

The reasons teams in the Philippines start evaluating alternatives to Google AppSheet are consistent, and they are rarely about features.

  • Your app needs genuinely relational data — linked records across customers, orders, inventory and staff — rather than a spreadsheet backend.
  • You need multi-step approval workflow with per-record permissions as a core feature, not an add-on.
  • You want implementation, training and support from a partner in Metro Manila rather than self-service documentation.
  • You work in English and Japanese and need both in the product and the support relationship.
  • You are not a Google Workspace organisation, so the bundled-Core advantage does not apply to you.

What Google AppSheet actually costs and requires

Entry pricingAppSheet Starter is US$5 per user per month and Core is US$10 per user per month. Enterprise Plus is published at US$20 per user per month.
The Google Workspace bundleAppSheet Core is included at no additional cost with most paid Google Workspace editions, including Business Plus and Enterprise. For Workspace shops building internal tools, the per-seat sticker price is often irrelevant.
What the free tier really isThe free tier allows building and testing with up to ten test users with no time limit. It is a prototyping tier, not a free production plan — real users require a paid plan.
Feature boundariesThe REST API, full automation and machine-learning features require Core. Single sign-on and advanced governance sit in the Enterprise tier, which is a common upgrade point for mid-market teams.
Public-facing appsApps shared publicly without sign-in need a Publisher Pro subscription, priced per app per month rather than per user.
Kintone's modelOne per-user monthly subscription including the relational database, workflow engine, permissions and REST API — no separate tier to unlock integration, and no spreadsheet backend to outgrow.

Where Google AppSheet remains the right answer

An honest alternative page has to say where the incumbent wins. Stay with Google AppSheet if any of these hold.

  • You already pay for Google Workspace Business Plus or Enterprise, so AppSheet Core costs you nothing extra.
  • Your apps are essentially structured data capture on top of Google Sheets, with light automation.
  • You need strong offline capability for field staff collecting data on mobile.
  • Your data already lives in Google Sheets or Drive and you want the shortest possible path from it to an app.

Moving the app layer to Kintone without leaving your existing stack

These two coexist comfortably. Plenty of Philippine businesses keep light field-data apps on AppSheet where Google Sheets is already the source of truth, and run the operational core — inventory, approvals, customer records, quality data — on Kintone. Kintone's REST API and webhooks let the two exchange data rather than compete.

Frequently asked questions about AppSheet and Kintone

Is Google AppSheet free?

There is a free tier, but it is a prototyping tier: you can build and test with up to ten test users with no time limit, and real production users require a paid plan. Separately, AppSheet Core is included with most paid Google Workspace editions, which is a genuinely free path for many Workspace organisations.

How much does AppSheet cost per user?

Starter is around US$5 per user per month and Core around US$10 per user per month, with Enterprise Plus published at US$20 per user per month. Publicly shared apps without sign-in use Publisher Pro, which is priced per app rather than per user.

Should we use AppSheet or Kintone in the Philippines?

If you already pay for Google Workspace Business Plus or Enterprise and your apps are spreadsheet-backed data capture, AppSheet is hard to beat on cost. If your apps need relational data, multi-step approvals, per-record permissions and local training and support, Kintone is the better fit.

Can Kintone and AppSheet run side by side?

Yes. A common arrangement keeps light field-data apps on AppSheet where Google Sheets is already the source of truth, and runs the operational core on Kintone, with data exchanged through Kintone's REST API and webhooks.

What happens when an AppSheet app outgrows Google Sheets?

That is the usual migration trigger. Spreadsheet backends struggle once records must relate to each other, approvals become multi-step, and multiple teams edit simultaneously. Kintone provides the relational structure, workflow and permissions as platform features rather than workarounds.
